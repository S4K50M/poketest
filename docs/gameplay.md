# Gameplay and controls

[Documentation index](README.md) · [Project README](../README.md)

## First expedition

Choose three distinct companions and begin at the Last Lantern. Move toward Professor Vell, use E to read the opening dialogue, and enter the Mourningwild portal. Restore guardian fragments to open the next chapter. Walking into a glowing Wellspring automatically restores every companion’s health and Focus and binds your respawn point. Camps provide utility services without healing. M opens the atlas; N opens quests, talents, equipment, bonds, covenants, and history.

Mobile screens require landscape. Portrait shows a rotate prompt on the home screen, in menus, and during gameplay; an active adventure pauses until the device rotates. Movement/aim is on the left; strike, jump, dash, Guard, heavy, special, and power are on the right. The left-pad SWAP button or party cards switch companions. Hold-to-heal and nearby interaction buttons sit beside movement; grapple appears after its unlock. The menu prioritizes recovery and loadouts, then exploration and tools. Keyboard controls are also listed in the in-game guide. Sound effects are optional and start muted.

## Party and survival

Each companion keeps independent hearts, Focus, and signature state; repeat attack cooldowns apply across the party. Switching preserves location, velocity, debuffs, and spent air resources. It cannot refill jumps or grant free invulnerability. A knocked-out companion yields to a living partner. A full party wipe opens **Game Over**, loses 10% of Lumens, and revives enemies throughout the gate where you died, including its bosses and minibosses. Other gates stay unchanged. Retry restores the party at your last activated Wellspring. Story milestones, abilities, and claimed unique rewards remain yours; revived enemies cannot pay their first-victory rewards again.

The eight supplied identities retain their different anatomy and elemental skills: the quadrupedal magma gator, mantis-wasp, glacier yeti, cloud griffin, rock beetle, shadow jackal, natural nose-lance shark, and chrome dragon. The new companions use separate deer, moth, and owl rigs:

- **Mycora:** fungal antlers, moss cloak, healing seed communion, and an armored briar rush.
- **Lumivern:** four moon-patterned wings, a lantern heart, seeking light needles, and a solar cone.
- **Chronowl:** six feathered wings, an hourglass crown, a stasis ring, and an ice dash.

Minions include shell enemies that need a heavy hit, static grubs that punish close attacks, burrowing ambushers, slippery water creatures, ranged thorn sprites, furnace bats, ink rays, storm birds, frost crawlers, and teleporting rift echoes. Guardians have 2.2 times their previous health and three phases, changing rhythm at two-thirds and one-third health. Twelve dimensional minibosses guard court seals and optional vaults. Their attacks combine charges, aimed projectile fans, delayed elemental sigils, leaping slams, and jumpable shockwaves. The final phase chains attacks and shortens recovery. Glass Marauders resist light strikes, Veilwasps attack from the air, Graveburrowers mark an eruption under you, and Chaincasters keep their distance while firing rooting bolts.

## Inventory and crafting

Defeating creatures and finding echoes awards Bond XP and Lumens. Bond levels improve party health and damage. Caches and ore provide regional materials; the Resonance Pick breaks ore and the Echo Lantern reveals hidden shrine rewards. Rewards persist across room changes and cannot be collected twice.

Craft flasks and four charms near camps, the Mender’s Workbench, or Wellsprings, including spent springs. Equip two charms: Feral Fang improves damage, Wellspring improves Focus regeneration, Swift Feather improves running and grapple range, and Briar Heart retaliates when hit. Heartwood forging offers three party-wide upgrades. Companion changes require a service point. Changing companions and traveling between camps do not supply a free heal.

The original campaign save records party health, progression, inventory, charms, discoveries, claimed rewards, defeated creatures, story notes, endings, and a room checkpoint. Reloading starts at that checkpoint; it does not preserve the exact midair position or a guardian’s partial health. Autosaves happen during play and at room changes, rewards, spring activation, crafting, death, and leaving. A saved pending death reopens Game Over after continuing. An older arena save is not used by the new campaign.

J, K, and L have shared-party repeat cooldowns of 0.2s, 0.6s, and 1.5s. Arts have individual timers plus shared cast recovery; Surge requires its gauge and a 15s cooldown. Time your next input rather than repeatedly tapping. Cooldowns survive swaps and reloads; the HUD and touch controls show remaining time.

## RPG progression

Bond XP improves party level, health, and damage. Two starting talent points let you begin a Fang, Heart, or Thread build. Additional points come from Bond levels, forbidden guardians, and cleared world events. Talents affect damage, execution, lifesteal, healing, inactive Focus recovery, survival, movement, loot collection, and synergy.

RPG equipment is separate from crafted charms: the party can equip **one relic and one armor item**, alongside **two crafted charms**. Relics and armor modify damage, defense, speed, Focus recovery, or Surge gain. Unequipped equipment can be salvaged once for Lumens. Ora sells finite stock at the sanctuary; purchases are not a renewable item farm.

Individual companion mastery rises through that companion’s attacks and kills. Ranks occur at 80, 200, and 400 mastery: Kindred, Soulbound, and Awakened. They add damage and visual auras. Reaching 200 on any companion also unlocks Kindred Choir in the Arsenal.

Direct hits charge Threefold Resonance. At 100%, R calls a protected multi-companion burst that restores Focus to living party members. Its own damage cannot immediately refill the entire gauge.

Talk to Iona at the sanctuary to begin the optional chronicle. Speak to quest givers to accept objectives and turn in ready quests; N lets you track one. World History collects revealed history and recovered field notes. The three covenant choices are permanent for that expedition; see the [world guide](world-and-story.md).

After fifteen seconds of simulation time in a region path, an uncleared world fracture can appear. Defeat its three echoes for Lumens, a talent point, and a flask. Completed events persist; leaving a partial event does not restore its defeated enemies for farming.

## Accessibility and presentation

Use H → Arsenal to toggle reduced motion. It removes camera shake and softens flashes; a new journey initially follows the browser’s motion preference. The sound button toggles optional synthesized effects. Pausing, opening a menu, losing window focus, or hiding the tab stops active gameplay through the UI.

For exact timing and art unlocks, see the [combat reference](combat.md). For checkpoint and storage limitations, see [save format](save-format.md).

## Dimensional expeditions

Each of the six sanctuary gates leads through a grounded tunnel road, a lower dungeon passage, and a warden court to the guardian’s chamber. The main routes have continuous floors instead of long runs of bottomless gaps. Optional upper climbs, high passages, and buried vaults branch off this route; wider landings and catch shelves make a missed jump recoverable. The high passage loops back toward the gate, while the vault has a stronger optional reaver and a sealed hoard. Earned double jumps, air dashes, wall climbing, and grapples open those optional routes. Read local inscriptions and use M to inspect discovered connections. There are no directional route arrows, and movement abilities do not bypass the court’s guardian seal.

Each dimension gate is one level and contains **two or three Wellsprings**, distributed among its interior chambers. Its first spring is on the lower dungeon floor, so recovery does not require completing an upper platforming route. Their locations vary with a new expedition’s saved world seed and remain stable across reloads. Enter the light while grounded to fully restore all three companions and Focus, with no E press or sitting animation. A spring works once per attempt; remaining beside it cannot repeatedly heal you. Death rearms the springs in that gate. The sanctuary hearth is reusable when you leave and return.

Camps replace the old healing benches. They support preparation and travel, but never refill health or change your respawn spring. Crossing a door changes the local fall checkpoint, not the last activated spring. Missing an ordinary upper jump now lands on a lower shelf or the tunnel floor, without a fall penalty. Leaving all authored ground still uses the existing nonlethal-fall rule: lose a heart and return to the local checkpoint. If the party is wiped, **Game Over** holds the simulation until you choose **Retry** or **Save & Title**. Retry returns to the last activated spring; a save made on Game Over still requires that choice after reload.

Main and forbidden guardian victories award one flask without fully healing the party; dimensional minibosses award materials instead. Base Focus healing still requires 72 uninterrupted frames and 40 Focus. Cave walls, low tunnel ceilings, rock shelves, roots, masonry, and local lighting frame the safer grounded passages. Combat damage, healing costs, and the shared movement physics are unchanged.
